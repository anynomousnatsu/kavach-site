German cockroach · साङ्लो

Small, fast, light brown, two dark stripes behind the head. The most serious kitchen pest in Kathmandu.

Signs you'd notice
Pepper-like droppings in drawer runners and hinge lines, a musty odour in enclosed cabinets, live insects scattering when a light goes on at night.
Why it's a risk for you
Restaurants: droppings and shed skins contaminate prep surfaces and stock; one sighting during service is a review, one during an inspection is a finding. Homes: allergen load is a documented asthma trigger in children.
Our protocol
  1. Torch inspection of every harbourage: runners, motor housings, voids, plinths
  2. Gel bait at each point — the colony feeds and dies, including the 90% you never see
  3. Non-repellent crack-and-crevice residual as a backstop
  4. Monitors placed, numbered, and read every visit
Covered by
Kitchen Shield · Home Shield · one-time gel treatment
Typical time to control
2–4 weeks to collapse an active colony; ongoing monitoring to keep reinfestation from next door in check.

American cockroach · ठूलो साङ्लो

Large, red-brown, flies in warm weather. Lives in drains, sewers and voids — Kathmandu's open drainage is its highway.

Signs you'd notice
Big roaches emerging from floor drains at night, sightings in bathrooms and basements, especially in monsoon.
Why it's a risk for you
It walks from the sewer to your prep table. The contamination route is direct and the sight of one during service is unforgettable — for the wrong reasons.
Our protocol
  1. Drain and void treatment at the breeding source
  2. Residual barrier at entry points: drain surrounds, pipe penetrations, thresholds
  3. Mesh drain covers recommended and quoted
  4. Monitoring at drains, logged monthly
Covered by
Kitchen Shield · Store Shield · Home Shield
Typical time to control
Immediate knockdown; sustained exclusion needs the monthly drain programme.

Bed bug · उडुस

Flat, apple-seed-sized, feeds on sleeping people. The most emotionally expensive pest in Nepal's hospitality sector.

Signs you'd notice
Rust-coloured spotting on mattress seams, cast skins in headboard joints, itchy bites in lines of three, a sweet almond smell in heavy infestations.
Why it's a risk for you
Guesthouses: one TripAdvisor mention costs a season. Homes: they spread through flats via shared walls and second-hand furniture, and DIY sprays make them scatter deeper.
Our protocol
  1. Steam every seam, joint and void — kills all life stages including eggs
  2. Targeted residual to interception points
  3. Day-10 follow-up timed to the hatch cycle
  4. Day-20 verification with monitors; adjacent rooms inspected
Covered by
Room Shield · Bed bug Gold treatment
Typical time to control
20 days to verified eradication with the 3-visit protocol. Guaranteed in writing.

House mouse · मुसा

Small, curious, needs only a pencil-width gap. Feeds little and often — which spreads contamination everywhere.

Signs you'd notice
Rice-grain droppings along walls and in drawers, gnawed packaging, greasy smear marks on skirtings, noises in ceilings at night.
Why it's a risk for you
A mouse contaminates far more than it eats — droppings and urine over prep surfaces and stock. In a DFTQC inspection, droppings are an instant finding.
Our protocol
  1. Entry survey: every gap, pipe run and door threshold photographed
  2. Tamper-proof bait stations placed, numbered, mapped
  3. Snap-trap supplement where bait is unsuitable
  4. Proofing quote — the permanent fix — in writing
Covered by
All programmes · rodent proofing
Typical time to control
1–3 weeks knockdown; permanent only with proofing.

Roof rat · मुसा (the climber)

Sleek, agile, travels on wires and roof lines. The rat you hear in the ceiling.

Signs you'd notice
Night noises overhead, droppings in roof spaces and store rooms, gnawed cables (a real fire risk), fruit and stock damage high up.
Why it's a risk for you
Hotels: roofline entry into upper floors and stores. Warehouses: stock loss and cable damage. It rarely comes to floor-level bait, which is why amateur baiting fails.
Our protocol
  1. Roofline and entry survey — we follow the smear marks
  2. Stations placed on the travel routes, elevated where needed
  3. Take recorded monthly, stations moved to follow activity
  4. Roofline proofing and netting quoted
Covered by
Room Shield · Store Shield · Kitchen Shield
Typical time to control
2–6 weeks; roof access points must be sealed for lasting control.

Norway rat · ठूलो मुसा (the burrower)

Heavy, burrowing, lives in drains and under floors. Kathmandu's open drainage keeps the pressure permanent.

Signs you'd notice
Burrow holes near foundations and drain lines, gnawed door bottoms, a large rat crossing the back lane at dusk.
Why it's a risk for you
Structural gnawing, sewer-to-kitchen contamination, and it breeds faster than a quarterly spray job can suppress. This is a monitoring-and-proofing pest, full stop.
Our protocol
  1. External burrow and drain survey
  2. Tamper-proof external station line — the first line of defence
  3. Internal stations only as backstop, all mapped and logged
  4. Drain grates and door sweeps quoted; burrows treated and collapsed
Covered by
Kitchen Shield · Store Shield
Typical time to control
3–6 weeks to suppress; permanent pressure means permanent monitoring.

Subterranean termite · धमिरा

Eats wood from the inside out, builds mud tubes up foundations. Peak swarming April–June.

Signs you'd notice
Mud tubes on walls and foundations, hollow-sounding timber, discarded wings on windowsills after a swarm, doors and frames that suddenly crumble.
Why it's a risk for you
Structural damage is slow, silent and uninsured. By the time you see a mud tube indoors, the colony has been feeding for months.
Our protocol
  1. Moisture-meter and probe inspection of all timber and foundations
  2. Post-construction: drill-and-inject perimeter barrier (NPR 20–25/sq ft)
  3. Pre-construction: foundation soil treatment (NPR 200–450/sq m)
  4. Annual re-inspection, on record
Covered by
Termite treatments — and tell your contractor about pre-construction, it's the cheap one
Typical time to control
Barrier is immediate; colony collapse over 4–12 weeks.

House fly · झिँगा

Breeds in waste and drains; peaks with the monsoon. The visible hygiene failure.

Signs you'd notice
You notice them. So do your customers, and so does the inspector — fly activity is the single most visible hygiene signal in a dining room.
Why it's a risk for you
Mechanical contamination from waste to food, and an immediate perception problem. Kathmandu's irregular waste collection makes the pressure structural.
Our protocol
  1. Insect light traps positioned away from food and windows
  2. Glue boards counted monthly — a fly index, trended
  3. Breeding source treatment: bins, drains, waste areas
  4. Door discipline and screening recommendations in the report
Covered by
Kitchen Shield · Room Shield · ILT units
Typical time to control
Counts drop within a month; season-long management through monsoon.

Drain fly · the small fuzzy one by the drain

Tiny, moth-like, hovers near floor drains and sinks. Breeds in the biofilm inside the pipe.

Signs you'd notice
Small fuzzy flies resting on bathroom and kitchen walls near drains, worse after a quiet weekend.
Why it's a risk for you
They emerge from exactly the place your customers least want to think about, and they signal an unmaintained drain to any auditor.
Our protocol
  1. Foaming bio-enzymatic drain treatment — strips the breeding film
  2. All drains treated, not just the complained-about one
  3. Monthly retreatment inside the programme; the film regrows
Covered by
Kitchen Shield · Home Shield
Typical time to control
1–2 weeks; monthly maintenance keeps them gone.

Mosquito · लामखुट्टे · dengue vector

Aedes aegypti breeds in clean standing water — flower pots, roof tanks, tyres. Dengue season June–October.

Signs you'd notice
Day-biting mosquitoes (dengue's Aedes bites in daylight, unlike the night-biting Culex), larvae wriggling in any standing water.
Why it's a risk for you
Dengue is now an annual Kathmandu epidemic. For hotels, guest illness is a reputational and duty-of-care issue; for homes, it's the family's health.
Our protocol
  1. Breeding-site survey — the part that actually matters
  2. Larvicide for tanks and water that can't be emptied
  3. Indoor ULV fogging (NPR 450/room), outdoor thermal fogging (NPR 4,000+)
  4. Written breeding-site report so it doesn't come back next week
Covered by
Seasonal fogging · Home Shield (monsoon visits)
Typical time to control
Immediate knockdown; sustained control needs breeding-site removal.

Ant · कमिला

Trails to sugar and grease; nests in wall voids and under floors. Annoying at home, a contamination vector in kitchens.

Signs you'd notice
Foraging trails along skirtings and worktops, fine soil at wall cracks, winged swarms in early monsoon.
Why it's a risk for you
Trails cross waste and food areas alike. Spraying a trail kills scouts and splits colonies — the classic way a small problem becomes three.
Our protocol
  1. Trail-back to locate the nest
  2. Colony-specific gel or granular bait — workers carry it home
  3. Void treatment where nests are inside walls
Covered by
Home Shield · Kitchen Shield
Typical time to control
1–3 weeks for full colony collapse via bait.

Stored product beetle · the one in the flour sack

Weevils and flour beetles that arrive inside grain, flour, rice and pulses — then colonise your whole store.

Signs you'd notice
Small beetles in flour or rice, webbing in packets, dusty residue under sacks, holed grains.
Why it's a risk for you
Warehouses: one infested lot condemns stock and accounts. Restaurants: an infested dry store contaminates everything you cook. Almost always brought in with a delivery.
Our protocol
  1. Pheromone monitoring traps in the dry store — early warning before you can see them
  2. Infested stock identified and isolated (we tell you which lot, with photos)
  3. Shelving and void treatment; incoming-goods checks written into your SOP
Covered by
Store Shield · Kitchen Shield (dry store monitoring)
Typical time to control
Immediate once the source lot is found; monitoring keeps the next delivery honest.

Birds — pigeon · परेवा

Roosting pigeons on ledges, rooflines, shafts and AC platforms. Managed by exclusion, not harm.

Signs you'd notice
Droppings streaking façades and balconies, nesting debris blocking gutters, mites migrating indoors from nests.
Why it's a risk for you
Droppings are corrosive to stone and paint, a slip hazard on walkways, and a respiratory concern in quantity. Hotels: it's the first thing a guest photographs from the balcony.
Our protocol
  1. Roost survey and netting/spike plan drawn on photos
  2. UV-stabilised netting (NPR 90–250/sq ft) or spikes (NPR 600–1,000/pc), installed at NPR 85/running ft
  3. Cleanup and disinfection of the roost area
Covered by
Bird netting & spikes — bundles with Room Shield
Typical time to control
Immediate and permanent where netted. Exclusion is the whole job.
